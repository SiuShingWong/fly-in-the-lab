In interphase each nucleus's two centrosomes move apart and gather more and more PCM, so your GFP signal grows. In mitosis the nuclear envelope breaks down, a spindle forms between the centrosomes, and the nuclei divide. Your goal: a time-lapse of at least one full cycle, with bright, sharp centrosomes and little bleaching.

1 · Find an embryoClick embryos for a quick fluorescence look. You want nuclear cycle 10–12: evenly spaced nuclei right at the surface. Each look costs that embryo a little light.
2 · FocusCoarse focus until the embryo glows, then fine focus until the centrosomes are crisp dots beside each nucleus.
3 · Camera and laserLaser power, exposure, z-slices and time interval. Bright enough to measure, never saturated, and inside your light budget.
4 · RecordWatch in transmitted light (free), start just after a division, keep the focus sharp as the embryo drifts, and stop once a full cycle is done.
5 · ResultsYour movie, the centrosome intensity curve, a score and tips.
